The entropy spectra of chaotic maps of an interval

Vinod Kumar P B

Relevance
Exact topological entropy spectra of interval dynamics are directly central.
TL;DR
The authors determine exact entropy spectra for several chaos classes of continuous interval maps and give constructions realizing all permitted values.
Problem
Determine which entropy values occur within Devaney chaotic, positive-entropy, Li–Yorke chaotic and mixing interval maps.
Main result
On the compact unit interval, the Devaney spectrum starts at half the logarithm of two inclusively, the positive-entropy spectrum at zero exclusively, and the Li–Yorke spectrum at zero inclusively; each also contains infinite entropy. Mixing maps exclude the half-logarithm endpoint, whereas Devaney maps with at least two fixed points have minimum logarithm of two. Thus a Devaney map attaining the half-logarithm minimum has a unique fixed point and is not mixing. On arbitrary intervals, the least-period spectra are described by Stefan constants, and Li–Yorke maps realize every nonnegative entropy including infinity.
Methods / framework
A truncation theorem showing that clipping to a compact subinterval creates no entropy. · A horseshoe argument for transitive maps with two fixed points. · Explicit piecewise-linear, infinite-entropy mixing and entropy-halving square-root constructions.

arXiv abstract Original arXiv PDF

Original abstract

For a continuous self-map f of a real interval L, let ent(f) denote the supremum of the topological entropies of the restrictions of f to its compact invariant sets; for a compact interval this is the usual topological entropy. For a class P of maps, the entropy spectrum of P is the set of values ent(f) taken by the maps f in P. We determine the entropy spectra of the three standard classes of chaotic maps of the compact interval [0,1]. Devaney chaotic maps have spectrum [(1/2) log 2, infinity], maps of positive entropy have spectrum (0, infinity], and Li-Yorke chaotic maps have spectrum [0, infinity]; thus every entropy compatible with the known lower bounds actually occurs, and the three spectra are strictly nested. Mixing maps have spectrum ((1/2) log 2, infinity], and Devaney chaotic maps with at least two fixed points have spectrum [log 2, infinity]. As a consequence, a Devaney chaotic map of the interval attains the minimal entropy (1/2) log 2 only if it has a unique fixed point and is not mixing. For an arbitrary interval, compact or not, maps with a point of least period n have spectrum [log sigma_n, infinity], where sigma_n are the Stefan constants, and Li-Yorke chaotic maps have spectrum [0, infinity]. The proofs combine a truncation theorem for maps of non-compact intervals, showing that clipping a map to a compact subinterval never creates entropy; a horseshoe argument showing that two fixed points force entropy log 2 on transitive maps; and explicit constructions, including an exact piecewise linear family, a mixing map of infinite entropy, and a square-root construction that halves the entropy of a transitive map.

Original abstract

We study properties of generic compact invariant subsystems of a fixed Smale space in the Hausdorff topology. For a transitive Smale space, we show that the hyperspace of all subsystems contains an open and dense family of NMC subsystems, a dynamically degenerate class with finite orbit structure. A substantially different picture emerges in the closure of the infinite transitive locus: minimality, unique ergodicity, and zero topological entropy are generic, while subsystems conjugate to regular Toeplitz shifts are dense. Despite generic zero entropy, typical subsystems exhibit large oscillations in complexity: we prove that they have infinite slow entropy at the polynomial scale, and their slow entropy at the stretched-exponential scale is equal to one. For mixing Smale spaces, we further study the closure of the infinite totally transitive subsystems. In this hyperspace, minimality, unique ergodicity, and zero entropy remain generic; the conjugacy class of any Sturmian subshift is dense; and uniquely ergodic subsystems whose invariant measure is weakly mixing form a residual set. In particular, topological weak mixing is generic in this space.

Original abstract

We present a unified treatment of various large deviation principles in the context of random walks induced by actions of countable groups. The fundamental notion undergirding the global perspective is that of weakly almost additive random walks, inspired by earlier works on the metric geometry of group actions as well as by recent developments in the theory of nonadditive thermodynamic formalism. We investigate the strong stable sets of the geodesic flow on hyperbolic surfaces and their relation to the injectivity radius.
