We study the structure of amenable subrelations of treeable measure-class-preserving equivalence relations, applying the Radon–Nikodym topography perspective initiated in our companion paper [TTD25]. Original abstract

The multiplicative ergodic theorem (MET) describes the asymptotic growth rates of -volumes under the iterates of an operator cocycle, i.e., compositions of operators driven by a stationary process, organizing the underlying space into equivariant filtrations or splittings into so called Oseledets subspaces. For operator cocycles in finite dimensions, and more generally in Hilbert spaces, one can control the geometry of the Oseledets subspaces given by the MET via the convergence rates of finite-time volume growth data. This makes the MET "effective" for matrix and Hilbert-space operator cocycles. We extend this effective theory to cocycles of Banach-space operators, obtaining quantitative angle estimates that are new even in the finite-dimensional setting. We take a sequential approach à la Raghunathan and Ruelle, allowing us to separate the geometric argument of the MET from measure-theoretic considerations. This permits weaker cocycle measurability hypotheses for the non-invertible and semi-invertible METs on a broad class of Banach spaces, including nonseparable ones. As an application, we establish the MET for transfer operator cocycles on associated with jointly measurable random Lasota-Yorke maps.
