We establish the full Ledrappier-Young entropy formula for ergodic invariant measures of diffeomorphisms when the positive Oseledets bundles and their nonpositive complement form a dominated splitting. Original abstract

We study the persistence of integrable structures in real-analytic nearly integrable systems under perturbations that may destroy some, but not all, of these structures. Specifically, we establish upper bounds on the numbers of real-analytic first integrals – quantities conserved by the motion – and commuting vector fields – infinitesimal generators of continuous symmetries – that can persist under the perturbation. These bounds are expressed in terms of Melnikov-type functions, which quantify the breakdown of the corresponding integrable structures. Unlike classical Melnikov theory, our conditions occur even in the absence of zeros of the Melnikov functions, so they do not necessarily imply the existence of homoclinic tangles. We give a geometric interpretation of these conditions in the context of the Arnold diffusion problem, showing that they imply micro-diffusion. We also obtain a criterion for higher-dimensional nearly integrable systems in which the dimension of the span of the Melnikov vectors restricts the numbers of possible first integrals and commuting vector fields. This shows that the geometry of the Melnikov vectors provides a new mechanism for detecting the breakdown of integrability. We give several applications to integrable Hamiltonian systems subject to forcing and dissipation.

Original abstract

We study two notions of pluripotency for dynamical systems. Strong pluripotency refers to the possibility of reprogramming, by arbitrarily small perturbations, the forward orbits of points in a set of positive Riemannian volume so that they track a prescribed orbit in a uniform time-averaged sense. In contrast, pluripotency, also called “statistical pluripotency" in this paper, requires only asymptotic agreement of empirical measures. Strong pluripotency implies statistical pluripotency, but the converse need not hold. For transitive Anosov diffeomorphisms, , we prove that strong pluripotency fails for every nonempty reference set. On the other hand, statistical pluripotency holds for a nonempty reference set if and only if it is contained in the basin of the unique SRB measure. This basin is invariant and has full Riemannian volume, but contains no periodic points. These results reveal a sharp distinction between the two notions even within uniformly hyperbolic dynamics.
