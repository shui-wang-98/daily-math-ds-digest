Our destination formula computes it as the closure of a unipotent orbit, determined by the Cartan data and a bounded Levi factor. Original abstract

We study various families of non-periodic configurations of symbols assigned to vertices of regular graphs. We restrict ourselves to such systems where considered configurations support a uniquely ergodic measure. Symbols can be interpreted as particles and we are concerned with configurations which minimize the interaction energy of particles, the so-called ground-state configurations. One of the main questions then is whether ground-state configurations are stable with respect to small perturbations of interactions. Such stability may be interpreted as a sort of rigidity of non-periodic structures such as tilings, substitution systems, Sturmian systems, and other uniquely ergodic non-periodic systems. We introduce a property of bounded fluctuations of finite patterns of non-periodic configurations, the so-called Strict Boundary Property. We discuss how relevant this property is for stability of non-periodic structures. Examples of Robinson's tilings, Thue-Morse, and Sturmian systems are presented. Some open problems are formulated.

Original abstract

We study embedded solitons (ES) in a fifth-order Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation near a reversible Hamiltonian saddle-centre bifurcation. The solitons correspond to homoclinic orbits to an equilibrium whose linearisation has a pair of real and a pair of purely imaginary eigenvalues. Existence of symmetric homoclinic near the bifurcation is a codimension-one phenomenon governed by a parameter-dependent Stokes constant associated with an inner equation. Using complex matching, we derive an oscillatory large-parameter asymptotic formula for the Stokes constant. This formula implies the existence of a countable sequence of zeros and gives their asymptotic distribution in parameter space. We also develop two complementary high-precision numerical procedures for evaluating the Stokes constant: one based on the late terms of the formal inner expansion and another on direct computation of the stable and unstable manifolds. The numerical results agree with each other and with the asymptotic prediction, confirming both the existence and the predicted locations of the embedded-soliton branches.

Original abstract

We prove that every compact finite-rank nilspace admits a continuous injective morphism into a toral nilspace of the same step, and hence into a connected filtered nilmanifold. Combining this embedding theorem with the general nilspace inverse theorem, we establish the Jamneshan-Tao conjecture and extend its conclusion to second-countable compact abelian groups. This yields an inverse theorem for Gowers norms on all such groups, in which the correlating functions factor through morphisms into nilmanifolds. The proof relies on substantial foundations developed in nilspace theory over the past decade, particularly the general nilspace inverse theorem and the representation of compact finite-rank nilspaces as quotients of free nilspaces.

Original abstract

Transfer operators provide a linear description of nonlinear dynamical systems by propagating observables or probability distributions. In reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, these operators are represented through conditional mean embeddings, whose regularized estimator coincides with the posterior mean of Gaussian process regression. Building on this correspondence, we formulate Gaussian Process Dynamic Mode Decomposition, a Bayesian transfer-operator method that combines sparse variational Gaussian processes with kernel-based Koopman and Perron–Frobenius operator approximation. The resulting framework provides a principled route to uncertainty quantification, scalable sparse approximations, and data-adaptive dictionary learning for prediction and spectral analysis. Additionally, the model-selection formulation naturally gives rise to a forward–backward operator structure, which establishes a connection between Bayesian transfer-operator learning and time-lagged canonical correlation analysis.
