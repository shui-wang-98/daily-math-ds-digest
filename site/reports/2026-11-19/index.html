Original abstract

The Lyapunov spectrum of a linear cocycle (or vector bundle) is the set of Lyapunov vectors with respect to all ergodic probability measures for the base topological dynamical system. This spectrum disregards trajectories that are atypical from the measure-theoretic viewpoint, and so it is in general insufficient to determine important properties of the cocycle, such as existence of dominated splittings. We resolve this deficiency by introducing a larger set which we call the uniform spectrum of the cocycle. This definition is akin to Misiurewicz and Ziemian's rotation set. We prove several properties of the Lyapunov and uniform spectra, including relations between them. Motivated by a theorem of DeWitt and Gogolev, we consider Hölder-continuous cocycles over hyperbolic homeomorphisms and then provide bounds for the uniform spectrum in terms of the Lyapunov spectrum. We exhibit additional connections to the literature.

Original abstract

Forecasting a high-dimensional nonlinear system from a few point sensors requires an estimate of the current state and a model of its evolution. Reduced-order models usually take the full state as input, so with only sparse sensors they require an auxiliary full-state estimate whose error is inherited by the latent representation. We instead build the latent state from sensor measurements alone, using a shallow recurrent decoder (SHRED) network that encodes a history of point measurements, and train an echo state network to advance that latent state autonomously. We show that the resulting latent dynamics replicate the long-term dynamical invariants of the underlying system rather than merely tracking its short-term trajectory. For the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation, the autonomous latent model reproduces the leading Lyapunov exponents of the full system from 25 of 128 state components, using a latent state with 6-14% as many components as the full state. In this paper we consider the Toda lattice under random, spatially inhomogeneous initial data, meaning that its initial momenta and interparticle spacings are independent and their laws may slowly vary in space. When the density is strictly positive (but sufficiently small), we identify the model's hydrodynamic limit under Euler scaling.
