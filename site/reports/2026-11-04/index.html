We prove that if the entropy of generating forbidden words of a subshift over a finite alphabet is strictly smaller than its topological entropy, then the subshift has only finitely many ergodic measures of maximal entropy, each of which is Bernoulli up to a finite period, and admits a characteristic measure of maximal entropy. We describe an algorithm that, given a postcritically finite quadratic polynomial, outputs a finite state machine whose regular language is the set of external arguments for all points on the Hubbard tree, generalizing an example of Milnor. Original abstract

We investigate conditions and obstructions for the existence of ergodic full measures in the context of countable observables on compact spaces. Extending the theory in a unified way, we are able to show a complete picture via thermodynamic formalism for symbolic, uniformly hyperbolic and related systems. We prove conditional variational principles for the topological entropy, showing good approximations by ergodic full measures, and we give conditions where we precisely get ergodic full measures. Original abstract

A two-species aggregation-diffusion system on the one-dimensional torus with antisymmetric cross-interactions modeling pursuit-evasion dynamics is studied. The self-interaction strength is used as a bifurcation parameter, and the linear stability of the homogeneous state is characterized by Fourier analysis. It is shown that non-reciprocal coupling enlarges the stability region. In a symmetric diffusion setting, exponential convergence to the homogeneous state is established for sufficiently weak self-interactions by means of an entropy argument, with a decay condition independent of the cross-interaction strength. Under suitable nondegeneracy and spectral assumptions, the first loss of stability is shown to occur through a Hopf bifurcation associated with a single spatial Fourier mode. A branch of time-periodic patterns is thereby obtained, along which the two species oscillate with a fixed phase difference. An explicit formula for the cubic Hopf coefficient is derived, and an essential dependence of the direction and stability of the bifurcating branch on the second spatial harmonic is identified. In the case of matching diffusion coefficients and interaction kernels, sufficiently strong non-reciprocity is shown to transform a subcritical stationary instability of the corresponding reciprocal system into a supercritical Hopf bifurcation with stable periodic solutions. An exchange-of-stability result is proved, and the theory is illustrated for Kuramoto, two-mode cosine, and top-hat interaction kernels.

Original abstract

We derive and rigorously prove the path-kernel formula for the linear response of stochastic differential equations, namely, the derivative of averaged statistics with respect to a model parameter. The parameter may affect the diffusion coefficient, as well as the drift coefficient and initial condition. No hyperbolicity assumption is required. The proof introduces a single change of variables for the state path that transfers part of the unstable path perturbation to differentiation of the probability kernel. We also derive a pathwise Monte Carlo algorithm for estimating the response and demonstrate it on the 40-dimensional noisy Lorenz–96 system.

Original abstract

We introduce RaNNDy, a randomized neural network framework for learning transfer operators and their spectral decompositions. By fixing the weights of the hidden layers and training only the output layer, RaNNDy achieves significant reductions in computational costs – often by two orders of magnitude – with similar accuracy compared to state-of-the-art deep learning methods. The method is less sensitive to hyperparameter tuning and provides a closed-form solution for the output layer weights that directly represent eigenfunctions of the learned operator. Moreover, it is possible to estimate uncertainties associated with the computed spectral properties via ensemble learning. We demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed approach using diverse benchmark problems, including stochastic dynamical systems and protein folding processes, highlighting the strengths but also weaknesses of RaNNDy.
