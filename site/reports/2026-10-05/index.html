Sturmian beta-shifts do not have typical periodic optimization

Wen Huang, Oliver Jenkinson, Leiye Xu, Yiwei Zhang

Relevance
Sturmian subshifts, specification, ergodic optimization and maximizing measures directly match the strongest symbolic-dynamics interests; selected among the ten full digests in this backlog batch.
TL;DR
Beta-shifts whose lexicographically largest point is Sturmian fail typical periodic optimization despite having specification. Each admits a nonempty open set of Lipschitz potentials with the Sturmian measure as the unique maximizing measure.
Problem
Determine whether typical periodic optimization holds for Sturmian beta-shifts, and whether specification suffices for this property. Here typical periodic optimization means that Lipschitz potentials with a unique periodic-orbit maximizing measure contain an open dense subset of the Lipschitz function space.

For each beta-shift whose lexicographically largest point is a Sturmian sequence, the authors exhibit a nonempty open set of Lipschitz functions, all having the Sturmian measure as their unique maximizing measure. They describe these as the first known beta-shifts without typical periodic optimization and the first known shift spaces with specification but without this property. Original abstract

A shift space is said to have typical periodic optimization (TPO) if the set of Lipschitz functions whose unique maximizing measure is supported on a periodic orbit contains an open dense subset of the space of Lipschitz functions. We show that beta-shifts whose lexicographically largest point is a Sturmian sequence do not have TPO: on each such beta-shift there is a non-empty open set of Lipschitz functions, all of which have the Sturmian measure as their unique maximizing measure. These are the first known examples of beta-shifts without TPO, and, since they have the specification property, the first known examples of shift spaces with specification but without TPO. The corresponding beta-transformations do not have TPO in any space of Hölder functions on the interval. The main ingredient in the proof of these results is a rigidity property of Sturmian subshifts: modulo constants and Lipschitz coboundaries, the space of Lipschitz functions on such a subshift is one-dimensional.
