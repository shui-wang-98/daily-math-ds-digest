Original abstract

We study the Hausdorff dimension of attracting sets of three-dimensional flows whose one-dimensional quotient dynamics is non-uniformly expanding. Our main assumption is that the quotient map admits a full-branch uniformly expanding Gibbs–Markov induced map. We first prove that every such attractor contains a hyperbolic horseshoe of Hausdorff dimension strictly larger than two. As a consequence, after an arbitrarily small perturbation, the associated dynamical Lagrange and Markov spectra have robustly non-empty interior for an open and dense set of -observables. We then establish intermediate dimension properties. Every admissible intermediate Hausdorff dimension is realized by an ergodic invariant measure supported on the attractor. Moreover, every intermediate stable and unstable dimension is realized by a strictly ergodic hyperbolic subsystem. The approach extends the dimension theory previously developed for geometric Lorenz attractors to more general non-uniformly hyperbolic attractors including singular-hyperbolic attractors, Rovella attractors, and broad classes of skew-product suspension flows modeled by intermittent and Benedicks–Carleson maps.

Original abstract

We study products of independent, identically distributed random matrices with an exponential moment and a simple top Lyapunov exponent, without assuming irreducibility. Outside an exceptional invariant subspace called the equator, we establish contraction on average for a weighted projective distance and a spectral gap for the associated Markov operator. We obtain a central limit theorem, a Berry–Esseen bound, deviation estimates near the mean, and an almost sure invariance principle both for the growth of a fixed vector outside the equator and for the operator norm. In this paper, we develop an abstract framework for the theory of uniform exponential attractors in non-autonomous dynamical systems. Original abstract

We prove that for every choice of five positive masses the planar five-body problem has finitely many central configurations up to similarity. This settles Smale's sixth problem for five bodies in the plane. Albouy and Kaloshin proved it for masses outside a closed algebraic subset of codimension two. We follow their method. Infinitely many central configurations would give an algebraic curve of complex solutions. At the places at infinity of the curve the valuations of the mutual distances must balance. We determine the possible valuation vectors at all positive masses, together with the mass conditions under which each can occur. We treat limits in which four bodies form a cluster of zero potential energy by expanding the potential, which is constant along the curve, at a place. This excludes, at every positive mass, one of the two diagrams in which such clusters occur, and part of the other. The same expansion excludes one further diagram at every positive mass. What remains is an exact analysis, by computer algebra, of the sets of mass conditions that can hold together at positive masses. Exact rational certificates show that the balance fails under each of them, except on a few one-parameter families of masses, which we treat separately. On the rays through and the certificates do not apply. On the first ray they cannot apply: there the complex equations have a continuum of solutions, as Albouy and Kaloshin observed. On these rays we count the real central configurations: there are 306 and 294 up to orientation-preserving similarity. The certificates, the programs that check them and a proof of the theorem in the Lean proof assistant are available.
