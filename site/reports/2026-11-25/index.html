Original abstract

We investigate global topological linearization with unbounded perturbations of sublinear growth. Under a spectral gap condition and a sufficiently small Lipschitz constant, we show that the displacements of both the conjugacy and its inverse from the identity satisfy the same growth bound as the perturbation. We obtain representations of the displacement in terms of stable and unstable orbit series that converge uniformly on any bounded set and give the inverse conjugacy by using the Banach fixed points theorem in a weighted function space.

We study the asymptotic behaviour of the number of isolated periodic points of a dominant rational self-map of a smooth projective variety. We first give an upper bound for this number in terms of the degrees of the iterates of the map, thereby extending a theorem of Dinh–Nguyen–Truong to arbitrary characteristic. Our approach is purely algebraic and bypasses the theory of densities of positive closed currents. We then establish the exact asymptotic growth of isolated periodic points of birational self-maps of surfaces over a field of characteristic 0. Original abstract

We introduce nonnuclear Bunce-Deddens algebras, defined as reduced crossed product C*-algebras associated with free odometer actions of nonamenable countable discrete residually finite groups on a Cantor space. These are nonnuclear counterparts to the generalised Bunce-Deddens algebras introduced by Orfanos. For large classes of groups, we show that they share many regularity properties, including real rank zero, stable rank one, strict comparison of positive elements, and admitting a unique tracial state. Original abstract

This paper develops an approach to uniform phase-volume contraction for prescribed dynamical systems. The main result is that every positive rate strictly below the invariant-measure bound can be certified in finite search time by a regularized Sobolev gradient flow. The resulting positive density ensures exponential contraction of the weighted phase volume with unit prefactor. For each fixed regularization parameter, the flow converges exponentially to a unique minimizer. As the regularization vanishes, these minimizers converge to the feasible potential of minimum Sobolev norm for a fixed feasibility margin. The certification procedure rests on a Holder estimate that converts a bound on the integral penalty into a pointwise bound on the weighted divergence over the entire invariant domain. This estimate yields a stopping criterion for the search. Finite termination is also established in growing finite-dimensional trial spaces for a staged scheme with exact gradient evaluations and controlled positive quadrature. The scheme requires neither a supplied feasibility margin nor a bound on the norm of a feasible potential. A disk example shows that the invariant-measure bound can be approached by smooth certificates even when no C^1 certificate attains it. Analytical properties of novel local D-Liouville, D-Yamabe, and D-Ricci evolutions driven by divergence penalties are established. For the unnormalized D-Yamabe flow, a scaling obstruction to exponential stability of stationary conformal factors is established. For the D-Ricci flow, the transformation law for the prescribed vector field under DeTurck pullback is derived. This law specifies how the vector field must transform when the gauge term is removed. Examples on the torus and sphere identify obstructions to smooth convergence of the geometric evolutions.

Original abstract

Above its instability threshold, a parametrically excited oscillator settles into one of two phase states. The response of these states to a second, much slower modulation of the same stiffness is investigated. In the rotating frame, this modulation periodically varies the detuning and drives the phase librations. When the modulation is slow compared with the libration frequency, the phase states follow adiabatically. As the two frequencies become comparable, this approximation breaks down and the driven libration resonates with the intrinsic one. This nonadiabatic regime is examined for a Duffing oscillator with nonlinear damping. The averaged equations describe a periodically perturbed planar system with integrable unperturbed dynamics, whose homoclinic loop is derived in closed form. The phase states persist as invariant tori supporting quasiperiodic motion. Their stability and period-doubling bifurcations are captured by a damped Mathieu equation, in which the modulation contributes to both parametric stiffness and parametric damping. A Melnikov analysis yields explicit thresholds for separatrix splitting and the onset of homoclinic intersections, which are corroborated by numerical computations of the invariant manifolds. Numerically it is revealed that these intersections arise well before sustained chaotic motion develops. The fate of the parametric oscillation depends markedly on the resonance condition: modulation at the libration frequency destroys the invariant tori and quenches the oscillation, whereas modulation at twice the libration frequency leaves it intact.
